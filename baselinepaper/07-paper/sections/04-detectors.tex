All detectors read the same windows and masks and output one or more scores per window, higher meaning more
anomalous (Table~\ref{tab:detectors}). The scoring function receives features and mask only: no split name, no
vehicle key and no label. \textbf{Every detector can see the window length.} The mask is an input channel of the
autoencoders, and for a one-row window every window statistic has zero spread. Length is therefore controlled in
the evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}), not by the features.

\subsection{Neural Detectors}

The convolutional autoencoder and its supervised variant were developed in our earlier single-seed studies and are
used unchanged. The 14 input channels are the 13 features and the mask; the loss is the mean squared error over
real rows. The reconstruction score of a window is the mean over features of the per-feature squared error over
its real rows. The second score is the Mahalanobis distance of the latent vector to the latent vectors of all
training windows of the regime~\cite{denouden2018improving}.

The LSTM autoencoder is a recurrent reference \emph{of matched size on our input}: same latent size (16) and a
parameter count within 1.8\% of the convolutional model (64{,}429 against 63{,}293). It is not a replication of a
published model; published LSTM autoencoders for this task use other sequence units and
features~\cite{alladi2021deep,vishwanath2026federated}. One architecture was fixed; nothing was tuned.

The three neural detectors share one training setup: Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight decay
$10^{-5}$, batch size 256, gradient clipping at norm 1.0, learning-rate halving after 5 epochs without
improvement, and early stopping after 10 such epochs with the best-epoch weights restored.

\subsection{Detectors on Window Statistics}

The four classical one-class detectors and the logistic regression read 65 window statistics: for each of the 13
features, the mean, standard deviation, minimum, maximum and last-minus-first value over the real rows. The
number of rows is not among them, but a one-row window has zero standard deviation and zero last-minus-first in
every feature. The statistics are scaled with the mean and standard deviation of the regime's whole training
pool. The one-class SVM and the kNN reference set use one fixed subsample of 20{,}000 training windows, drawn with
the run's seed; the other detectors are fitted on every training window. Parameters are the defaults of
scikit-learn 1.9.1 where one exists.

\subsection{Model Selection}

Nothing is tuned on the test set, and no hyperparameter search was run. Model selection uses the validation pool
of the regime only: the lowest validation reconstruction loss for the autoencoders, and the highest validation
AUROC (benign against GridSybil) for the supervised autoencoder. In the mix regimes the validation pool contains
unlabeled GridSybil windows, like the training pool, so no benign-only validation signal enters these runs. The
classical detectors use no validation data. All hyperparameters in Table~\ref{tab:detectors} were written down
before any of these detectors scored a test split.

\subsection{Disclosure of Prior Test Exposure}\label{sec:exposure}

The protocol is not blind for two detectors. The convolutional autoencoder and the supervised model were developed
earlier, and seed-0 results of the benign-only autoencoder \emph{on this same test set} were known when the
protocol was written: window AUROC \prelim{0.736\,/\,0.391\,/\,0.973\,/\,0.611} for GridSybil\,/\,DataReplay\,/\,%
DoSRandom\,/\,DoSDisruptive. The supervised model had been evaluated on a different split. The architecture and
training settings of both are frozen as they were. The reference detectors (LSTM autoencoder, PCA, Isolation
Forest, one-class SVM, kNN, logistic regression) had no exposure to any test result. Any advantage from this
exposure therefore favors the convolutional autoencoder in comparison C5.
