\subsection{Preliminary Evidence From Earlier Studies}\label{sec:res-prelim}

Before this protocol we ran nine small studies with the same family of convolutional autoencoder and one with a
masked autoencoder (Section~\ref{sec:det-mae}). They are \emph{not} results of this paper: they use different
window lengths (32, 48, 64), different splits and test vehicles, and different normalizations
(Table~\ref{tab:prelim}, blue). Their numbers cannot be compared across rows, except between rows on the same
windows (the masked and the convolutional autoencoder at each $T$). Each autoencoder cell has been trained with
four seeds (0--3); we report the mean and sample standard deviation over seeds, without intervals. Seed~0 ran on
an Apple GPU (MPS) and seeds 1--3 on an NVIDIA T4 (CUDA), except for the benign-only autoencoder at $T=64$
(seeds 0--2 on MPS, seed~3 on CUDA) and the masked autoencoder (all seeds on MPS), so the seed spread also
contains a device change. The single-seed value 0.689 that an earlier draft gave for the benign-only autoencoder
at $T=48$ was the best of the four seeds; the four-seed mean is \prelim{0.682 $\pm$ 0.006}.

These studies motivated the regimes and the length control. Reconstruction trained on benign data scored
\prelim{0.657--0.734} on GridSybil across the three window lengths, an unlabeled mix scored
\prelim{0.482--0.485} where the attack is the majority of the training windows and \prelim{0.612} where it is
31\%, and GridSybil labels gave \prelim{0.928--0.929}. Reconstruction is stable across seeds (standard deviation
at most 0.006); the Mahalanobis score of the latent is the least stable score and near or below 0.5 on GridSybil in every cell (from \prelim{0.322 $\pm$ 0.015} for the
benign-only model at $T=64$ to \prelim{0.505 $\pm$ 0.012} at $T=32$). On
DataReplay and DoSDisruptive the benign-only autoencoder (\prelim{0.387 $\pm$ 0.010}, \prelim{0.598 $\pm$
0.012}) scored below window length alone (0.952, 0.967), and on DoSRandom it matched it (\prelim{0.967 $\pm$
0.005}). The contrast between the 31\% and the 64\% mix is suggestive only: these runs also differ in window
length, input, split and test vehicles. The controlled version is the mix-all and mix-gridfolders regimes of this
protocol (C2, C3), which are themselves two confounded points; a sweep over the attack share is out of scope
(Section~\ref{sec:scope}).

\textbf{Masked autoencoder.} Trained on the same benign windows as the benign-only convolutional autoencoder at
each $T$, the masked autoencoder (TabMlp encoder) scores lower on GridSybil at every window length:
\prelim{0.531 $\pm$ 0.002}, \prelim{0.607 $\pm$ 0.001} and \prelim{0.657 $\pm$ 0.002} at $T=64$, 48 and 32,
against \prelim{0.657}, \prelim{0.682} and \prelim{0.734} for the convolutional model, and at $T=64$ it is at
the level of length alone (0.535). Its full-reconstruction score gives \prelim{0.539}, \prelim{0.603} and
\prelim{0.650}; its Mahalanobis score is below 0.5 in every case (GridSybil \prelim{0.398--0.408}). On the
pseudonym-rotating attacks at $T=32$ its pooled masked-reconstruction AUROC is \prelim{0.693 $\pm$ 0.003}
(DataReplay), \prelim{0.988 $\pm$ 0.001} (DoSRandom) and \prelim{0.851 $\pm$ 0.006} (DoSDisruptive). The first
and third of these mostly measure window length (Table~\ref{tab:lmprelim}, Section~\ref{sec:disc-length}).
With the TabResnet encoder the masked autoencoder scores higher than with TabMlp at every window length,
\prelim{0.595 $\pm$ 0.003}, \prelim{0.650 $\pm$ 0.002} and \prelim{0.696 $\pm$ 0.001} on GridSybil
(length-matched \prelim{0.578}, \prelim{0.640} and \prelim{0.678}), but stays below the convolutional model at
each $T$; its full-reconstruction score gives \prelim{0.605}, \prelim{0.648} and \prelim{0.685}, and its
Mahalanobis score is again below 0.5 (\prelim{0.368--0.381}). At $T=32$ it scores \prelim{0.695 $\pm$ 0.008}
on DataReplay, \prelim{0.994 $\pm$ $<$0.001} on DoSRandom and \prelim{0.859 $\pm$ 0.004} on DoSDisruptive;
matched on length these become \prelim{0.559}, \prelim{0.988} and \prelim{0.734}, so DataReplay is again close
to chance, and DataReplay and DoSDisruptive stay below length alone in the pooled AUROC.

\textbf{Length-matched AUROC.} Table~\ref{tab:lmprelim} gives the pooled and the attack-weighted length-matched
AUROC (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}) for the two studies whose per-window test scores were saved with the window
length: the masked autoencoder (both encoders, four seeds each) and, as context only, one seed of a TimesNet
encoder~\cite{wu2023timesnet} that we pretrained self-supervised (no label in the loss) on the same benign
$T=32$ windows (the
self-supervised encoder of the thesis this paper comes from; scores: Mahalanobis distance and mean cosine distance
to the five nearest benign training latents, ``kNN''), next to the same encoder with random weights. On GridSybil
matching changes little: \prelim{0.657 $\to$ 0.631} for the masked autoencoder, \prelim{0.886 $\to$ 0.875} for
the TimesNet kNN score against \prelim{0.839 $\to$ 0.836} with random weights. On the pseudonym-rotating attacks
it changes the reading: the masked autoencoder's DataReplay score falls from \prelim{0.693} to
\prelim{0.490} (TabResnet: \prelim{0.695} to \prelim{0.559}), and the TimesNet Mahalanobis score falls from \prelim{0.946--0.960} to \prelim{0.477--0.579},
where the random-weight encoder scores as high or higher (\prelim{0.620--0.951}). The TimesNet numbers are one
seed and not a result of this paper. The per-window scores of the convolutional autoencoder cells were not all
saved, so its length-matched AUROC is not in the table.
