Question: how much does the training data matter?

One small model family. Three kinds of training data. Three window sizes. Is the result stable over seeds?

One-class

Train on benign windows only. Score = reconstruction error.

Unlabelled

Train on benign + GridSybil mixed. No labels used. Score = reconstruction error.

Supervised

Train with GridSybil labels. Score = classifier head output.

Model

Small 1-D ConvAutoencoder, latent z = 16. 37k–80k parameters.

Windows

T = 64, 48 or 32 messages from one sender, padded, with a mask.

Metric

GridSybil vs benign, test windows, AUROC. 0.5 = chance.

Seeds 0–3 per cell (supervised T64: 0–2). Mean ± sample sd over seeds. Slides 13–16: a one-class masked autoencoder (different model family).

The question is simple. Does the training data or the window size change detection more? And is one seed enough to say it? The model is the same small convolutional autoencoder in all cells. Only GridSybil is the positive class in this grid.

Length-only AUROC is computed on each cell's own test windows. It is the same for all seeds of a cell.

The length-only score needs no model: shorter windows are called more suspicious. On GridSybil it is only 0.53 to 0.56. Every one-class and supervised cell is above it. The unlabelled cells at T64 and T48 are below it. A likely reason: with 64% attack windows in training, the attack looks normal to the autoencoder.

This is the main warning for this model. Masked reconstruction is much harder on very short windows, so the score is high for short windows even when they are benign. GridSybil keeps most of its score after matching, so that part is not length.

Findings

  1. Seeds are stable. Headline sd ≤ 0.006 in every cell. The single-seed numbers hold, except one-class T48 (0.689 → 0.682).
  2. The training regime makes the biggest difference (cells differ in input and split, so not a strict ranking). Supervised ≈ 0.93 at every T. One-class 0.66–0.73. Unlabelled 0.48–0.61.
  3. Unlabelled training does poorly when attacks are common. At T64 / T48 (64% attack windows) it is below chance and below length; T32 (31%) is better, but its input also differs, so this is suggestive only.
  4. Only GridSybil is a real test. On DataReplay and DoS, window length alone does as well or better.
  5. Mahalanobis on z is weak for the autoencoders without labels: near or below chance.
  6. A masked autoencoder does not beat the ConvAE. Same one-class windows: 0.531 / 0.607 / 0.657 vs 0.657 / 0.682 / 0.734. Its score partly tracks window length.

Side experiments, GridSybil vs benign only. Not a ranking (next slide).

Six points. The seeds confirm the earlier single-seed picture, with one correction for one-class T48. Labels give a big gain. Unlabelled training breaks when the attack is common. The other three attacks are mainly a length effect. And the latent distance score is not useful. Last, the masked autoencoder is weaker than the ConvAE, and its high DataReplay score is a length effect.

Caveats

  • Not a ranking. Cells differ in input, split, normalisation and test vehicles. Read rows, not exact differences.
  • Two machines. Seed 0 on the Mac (MPS), seeds 1–3 on EC2 (CUDA); T64 one-class and supervised seeds 0–2 on the Mac. Not an exact reproduction.
  • Old number. One-class T48 0.689 was the best of four seeds. Quote 0.682 ± 0.006.
  • Supervised T64 has seeds 0–2 only (n = 3), and a fixed 0.5 threshold.
  • Rounded seed 0. Unlabelled T48, one-class T32 and supervised T48 / T32: seed 0 is a stored notebook value (3–4 dp).
  • Do not pool. EC2 reruns of one-class T64 seeds 0–4 give recon 0.6578 ± 0.0040 (n = 5). They are new runs; never mix them with the Mac seeds.
  • F11. DataReplay / DoS rotate pseudonyms every 1–2 messages, so their windows are 1–3 rows.
  • F14. In the T32 all-folder splits, GridSybil_0709 near-copies can sit in train and test. Not measured here.
  • F1. The four scenario families reuse the same traffic. Benign windows repeat across families.
  • Masked AE. One encoder (TabMlp), Mac only. Pooled DataReplay 0.693 is length, not detection (length-matched 0.490).

Every run was re-checked by an independent CPU recompute (judge): PASS or PASS-WITH-NOTES, no FAIL (EC2 seeds).

These caveats matter for any claim. The most important one: the columns are not comparable in a strict sense. Also, the EC2 five-seed run of the T64 one-class model is a separate set of runs and must stay separate from the Mac seeds.
