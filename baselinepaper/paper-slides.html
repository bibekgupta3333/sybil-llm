A Sybil attacker is one vehicle that a receiver sees as several

Vehicles in a V2X network broadcast position, speed and acceleration in periodic safety messages, and neighboring vehicles act on that content. Credentials show that a sender is enrolled; they do not show that the content is true.

The GridSybil case: several ghost vehicles at the same time. The other three attacks change pseudonym after one or two messages.

Four Sybil attacks in VeReMi-Extension (A16–A19)
GridSybilseveral pseudonyms at the same time, claimed positions a few meters apart; each pseudonym is kept for many messages
DataReplaySybilre-broadcasts data taken from a genuine vehicle, under a pseudonym changed after one or two messages
DoSRandomSybilsends at a higher rate than normal, with random field values; new pseudonym with almost every message
DoSDisruptiveSybilsends at a higher rate, with content taken from other vehicles' messages; pseudonyms used once or twice

Attack descriptions follow our own inspection of the logs; still to be checked against the dataset paper's definitions.

A Sybil attacker sends under several pseudonyms, so a receiver sees several apparent vehicles, for example a traffic jam that does not exist. Misbehavior detection checks the plausibility of what a vehicle receives. VeReMi-Extension has four Sybil attacks. Three of them change pseudonym every one or two messages, which matters later.

Published results are high, and the protocols behind them vary

0.93–0.99

Most one-class detectors on VeReMi data report 0.93–0.99 (accuracy, F1 or AUC)

76 papers surveyed. One benign-only detector under a stricter pipeline reports accuracy 0.824 (Campos et al. [9]).

To our knowledge no VeReMi paper among them

holds out vehicles with the traffic reuse in mind

Neither dataset paper defines a train/test split; where one is described, it is usually drawn over windows or sequences.

controls for window length

Among the papers we read, none reports a length or message-rate control.

compares kinds of training data under one common setup

“Unsupervised” almost always means benign data selected with the ground-truth labels.

When does reconstruction detect a Sybil?

The literature reports very high scores for one-class and autoencoder detectors. We surveyed 76 papers and read 22 of the 51 vehicular ones in full; the evaluation protocols differ a lot. The 0.93 to 0.99 range mixes accuracy, F1 and AUC from our notes and is not yet checked against every full text, so it is not on the same scale as our AUROC numbers. We do not claim the published numbers are inflated: we measure what reconstruction reaches with vehicles held out.

One overall question, three research questions

Overall questionHow well does the same small autoencoder separate Sybil traffic from benign, depending on what it is trained on?

Q1 · How well does it work?

Reconstruction error per Sybil type, with vehicles held out and thresholds from validation.

Q2 · Does the training data matter?

Benign only, an unlabelled mix, or labelled data.

Q3 · How much is a shortcut?

How far the number of messages in a window alone separates attack from benign.

When does reconstruction detect a Sybil?

The expectation is that what the model is trained on matters more than its details, and that for the three pseudonym-rotating attacks window length explains most of the score. The studies shown here were not controlled against each other, so they can suggest this but not establish it.

Evaluation: held-out vehicles, validation-only thresholds, a length control

Held-out vehicles

Every split is by sender vehicle. Test vehicles are never used for training, early stopping or thresholds.

Validation only

Early stopping, learning-rate schedule and thresholds come from validation data: for the one-class autoencoder the 95th and 99th percentile of benign validation scores.

Metrics

Window AUROC with the attack as the positive class; recall and benign false-positive rate at the validation threshold where reported.

Length-only control

Score = minus the number of messages in the window, no model. Reported next to the models, except in the overlapping-window study.

Second score

Latent distance: the Mahalanobis distance of z to the training z, reported next to the reconstruction error where computed (not for the unlabelled autoencoder at T = 48 or the supervised autoencoder at T = 48 and 32).

Seeds

Three seeds for the one-class autoencoder and the supervised autoencoder at T = 64, one seed for all other models.

The studies were run one after another with small differences in settings; they are not one controlled experiment. At T = 64 the one-class autoencoder was retrained with a longer epoch cap after a first scoring (0.645), and the length-only direction was first chosen on test labels (0.535, unchanged with the fixed rule).

The unlabelled autoencoder takes its thresholds from all validation scores, without labels. The supervised autoencoder at T = 48 and 32 takes its thresholds from validation as well; at T = 64 the threshold is fixed at 0.5. Single-seed runs have no confidence interval.

What the early numbers mean, and what limits them

Reading, to be tested under one common setup
  • Replay is in-distribution. Seen from one receiver and one pseudonym, a replayed window is a genuine trajectory under a new pseudonym.
  • Length is a signature and a shortcut. A burst of short-lived pseudonyms is real evidence of these attacks; it tells nothing about whether a model has learned plausible motion.
  • The unlabelled autoencoder learns what is frequent. Where GridSybil is 64.6% of the training windows (unlabelled autoencoder, T = 64), it is the majority class and “normal” is redefined.
Limitations
  • The numbers are preliminary: mostly single-seed runs that are not controlled against each other.
  • At T = 32 (all four attack scenarios), near-copies of some GridSybil 0709 cars may straddle the split; not measured.
  • At T = 32, benign traffic appears once per attack family, about four times in total, so benign windows are not independent.
  • The gap to published numbers is reported as an observation; no cause is claimed.
  • The 31% against 65% contrast is suggestive only: the two runs also differ in window length, input, split and test vehicles.

Preliminary. To be repeated under one common setup before any of this is claimed.

What can reveal a replay is context across senders, for example two pseudonyms claiming the same track; no model in these studies has that input. The published values are accuracy, recall or F1 at a chosen threshold on other sequence units, and the studies differ from ours in the split unit, the threshold source and the sequence unit, none of which we varied.

Conclusion, as far as it can be drawn today

  1. Fixed already, because it needs no model: the number of messages in a window alone separates DataReplay and both DoS attacks from benign traffic with an AUROC of 0.952–0.967, and GridSybil with 0.559 (T = 32).
  2. Observation: across the kinds of training data, AUROC differs by 0.32–0.45 at a fixed window length (0.13–0.21 between the two reconstruction-based rows); across window lengths by at most 0.13 within one kind. Neither comparison is controlled.
  3. Early run, one seed: the one-class autoencoder does not separate replayed trajectories from benign ones: 0.391 over all windows and 0.349 on the 3,883 windows with 4 or more messages, both below chance.
  4. Recommendation: evaluations on this dataset should hold out vehicles across scenario families, state how the training data was selected, and report a length-only score next to every sequence model.

Preliminary. Points 2 and 3 come from runs that are not controlled against each other; they are to be repeated under one common setup.

Only the first point is final. The others are what the baseline studies show; a repeat under one common setup can confirm or overturn them.

Suggested additions and why they strengthen the paper

1 · Attack-share sweep

What: train the same autoencoder with 0, 1, 5, 20, 50 and 64% GridSybil windows in the unlabelled training pool, everything else fixed.

Why: turns the two confounded points (31% and 65%) into a controlled curve, so “more attack traffic in unlabelled training, worse reconstruction” becomes a measured result instead of a suggestion; the low end (1–5%) is the realistic case for data collected in the field.

Cost: about one day on the existing code.

2 · Deep SVDD as a further model

What: the standard deep one-class method [Ruff et al., 2018], trained on the same windows and training data.

Why: answers “is the weak result specific to reconstruction?” If a one-class method that does not reconstruct is also weak, the finding is about the one-class assumption, not about our autoencoder.

Cost: to be estimated.

3 · Gradient-boosted trees on window statistics

What: a boosted-tree classifier on the same window statistics the logistic regression uses, with GridSybil labels.

Why: a strong, widely used tabular baseline; shows whether the neural supervised model is needed at all, and matches what related work uses (Fdil and Souidi, 2024).

Cost: to be estimated.

What it takesSettings are frozen before any result is looked at; same seeds and test set as the other models; the runs go to the cloud with the rest.

Proposals. None is run or approved; not part of the thesis plan.

These are proposals; none is run or approved. They are the three additions with the best return for the December paper. The sweep comes first: it turns the most original observation into a measured curve for about one day of work.
