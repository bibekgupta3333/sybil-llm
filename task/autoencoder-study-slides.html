The benign model's error is dominated by noisy acceleration

  • Acceleration is hardest (0.367 / 0.335); positions almost exact.
  • Test, benign vs GridSybil: log Δτ 0.130 vs 3.563, range 0.039 vs 1.125.
  • One mean over 13 features lets acceleration noise hide these.

Study D, mean masked MSE per feature · DoSRandom: range 8.600, claimed_vel_x 3.356 (out-of-range claims)

Where does D's error come from? Acceleration dominates the benign error. GridSybil differs most in message timing and range, but those signals are diluted by averaging over all 13 features. That points to a per-feature scaled score.

Lessons and next steps

Learned

  • Training data defines normal: 0.484.
  • Benign only, no labels: 0.736.
  • Labels: ≈ 0.93 at T48 and T32.
  • Error alone weak: 0.478 / 0.598.
  • DoS / DataReplay: 1–3-message windows (F11).

Next

  • 3 seeds per study.
  • Per-feature scaled error.
  • Message-level scores vs the paper.
  • Co-movement across pseudonyms.
  • TimesNet: benign-only pretraining, LR fix.

The pipeline held: vehicle splits, labels read last, val thresholds gave the intended ≈ 5% benign alarms

The main lesson: what the model treats as normal matters more than how windows are cut. Benign-only training is the label-free route, and it fits the thesis pretraining plan. Next: more seeds, a better score, and a message-level comparison with the paper.
