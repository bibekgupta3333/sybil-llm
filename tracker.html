Self-Supervised Trajectory Foundation Model for Sybil Attack Detection — VeReMi-Extension · Master's Thesis, Florida Polytechnic University

Core Research Objective
Detect Sybil attacks in vehicular networks — one adversary forging many ghost-vehicle identities — by pretraining a compact Transformer on unlabeled trajectory windows with two self-supervised objectives (Masked Trajectory Reconstruction + Trajectory Consistency Prediction over synthetic corruptions), then transferring the frozen/fine-tuned encoder to detection under few-shot, zero-shot, and cross-scenario evaluation.
■ 285,926 windows of 20 steps × 13 kinematic features ■ 4 Sybil attack families + benign (5-class) ■ two disjoint scenarios (0709 / 1416) for transfer ■ label-efficiency is the headline claim

Two data facts that govern everything downstream. (1) Windows from one sender overlap (stride 10 < window 20), so any split that is not sender-level leaks half-windows across the boundary — the committed splits are sender-level, and any new split must be too. (2) Attack labels are per-sender, not per-window — an attacker's individual windows can look locally benign, which is both the motivation for sequence modeling and a source of label noise the thesis must disclose (plan 6.6).

Why "road-network-grounded" is legitimate
Honest trajectories are road-constrained by the generator. We hold no road graph — the grounding is in the data.
If asked "is the ground truth noise-free?" No — matched messageIDs are byte-identical between ledger and receive logs. Our windows are what an eavesdropping detector sees. That is the right realism.

Sentinel
dt = dpos = dspd = 0 only at step 0 of each identity's first window (its first message has no predecessor) — ≈ 8.8% of windows. Later windows carry real deltas at step 0. Mask it out of the MTR loss.
Why deltas matter: forgeries are self-inconsistent (frozen position + moving speed; 1 km jump in 1 s). Inconsistency is learnable without labels.

So what

If asked "aren't heading and speed redundant?" Partly, for honest vehicles — that redundancy is the detector. Keep both.

Professor Q&A · data

Likely questions — one-line answers

QuestionAnswer
Is the dataset balanced?By window, binary is 50/50. By class it's up to 9.5:1 (benign : DataReplay). By identity, 61/39. I report all three and use macro-F1.
Why are attack families different sizes?Same ~1,700 attacker vehicles each; they differ in how many sustained fake identities survive the 20-message cutoff. DoS pairs are identical by construction.
Why not per-message classification?Speed/acceleration marginals are identical across 3 of 4 families (slide 8). The signal is cross-step inconsistency — a sequence model's job.
Is it real data?Simulated (SUMO/LuST + VEINS). Realistic road constraints, but smoother than telemetry — stated as a limitation; *_noise channels reserved for a robustness check.
How do you know there's no leakage?Identity-level splits, zero shared identities on every pair, train-only normalization — all re-verified by scripts/audit_splits.py. Plus pretraining is restricted to idx_train.
Why is 0709 twice the size of 1416?Morning traffic density (~60k vs ~30k msgs/bin). Attack share is steady in both — so transfer measures context robustness.
Any data problems?One: a pseudonym sentinel splices 653 GridSybil vehicles into 14,764 windows (5.16%). Found in Phase 1, fix scheduled before any cited run.
Professor Q&A · method & labels

Likely questions — one-line answers

QuestionAnswer
You say no labels — where do labels appear at all?Only to score held-out windows, and optionally to fit a small head (probe or K-shot) on train identities. The encoder never sees one.
Doesn't pretraining on attack windows teach the model that attacks are normal?It sees them as trajectories, yes. Detection then rests on physical consistency, not frequency — which is why TCP's corruptions mirror real forgery physics.
What is the zero-shot protocol?An anomaly score from unlabeled train embeddings, threshold chosen on val. A benign-only memory bank would use labels, so we dropped it.
Why a transformer over a GRU/LSTM?Attention compares any two steps directly — replay and frozen-position patterns are non-local within the window. (And it's the ablation we will run.)
How will you handle imbalance without labels?You can't in pretraining — and needn't. It acts through exposure; I control it at the head (balanced probe, K per class) and in metrics (macro-F1, per-class recall).
Which family will be hardest?DataReplay (rarest, visually subtle) and DoSDisruptive (signature is sparsity/timing, which none of the four corruptions target — adding a timing corruption).
What is "road-network-grounded" if you have no road graph?The generator constrains honest motion to LuST roads; the representation inherits that implicitly. I claim implicit grounding, not a graph encoder.

Results status — no v1 number is citable as a thesis result. The pilot (models/transformer_model.ipynb → models/results/results_summary.json) establishes that the pipeline trains end to end and the representation carries signal. It supports no comparative claim, for five reasons that each independently disqualify it:
  • No control. There is no from-scratch run, so the number measures "a transformer on VeReMi," not "pretraining helps."
  • TCP degenerate. The consistency task only ever saw identity corruptions — the pretraining that ran is not the pretraining the thesis describes.
  • Single seed, 5 epochs. No variance estimate; almost certainly undertrained.
  • Precision/recall asymmetry unexplained. 0.992 vs 0.824 — the model misses ~18% of attacks; per-class and per-attack-family breakdown does not exist yet.
  • Environment unpinned. The torch training env has no committed freeze; the run is not reproducible by a third party today.
What the pilot legitimately shows: the loss decreases (training_loss.png), the classifier beats chance by a wide margin, and the t-SNE (embeddings_tsne.png) separates classes visibly. That is a green light for the plan, not a result.
