Self-Supervised Trajectory Foundation Model for Sybil Attack Detection — VeReMi-Extension · Master's Thesis, Florida Polytechnic University · self-supervised pretraining in progress (TimesNet SSL; first trial on benign + GridSybil), few-shot fine-tuning deferred

Core Research Objective
Detect Sybil attacks in vehicular networks — one adversary forging many ghost-vehicle identities — by pretraining a TimesNet encoder on network-heard pseudonym sequences without attack labels, using three self-supervised objectives (masked reconstruction, physics heads P1–P3 that detect violations we inject, and SimCLR-style contrastive learning with grid-cell hard negatives), then measuring how well the frozen encoder detects attacks and how few labels it needs (few-shot fine-tuning) and whether it transfers 0709 ↔ 1416.
■ network-heard sequences of T = 64 messages × 13 features (1 Hz) ■ 4 Sybil families + benign ■ two scenario groups (0709 / 1416) for transfer ■ label efficiency is the headline claim
Adopted plan (2026-10-05): stage1-plan.html (interactive pretraining / fine-tuning WBS) · docs/plan/clarification.md (the plan and what changed) · docs/plan/stage1-ssl-wbs.md (findings F1–F10, decisions D1–D9). TCP is dropped; TimesNet only in the code (D11; a Transformer can return later as an ablation).
Scope of the current work (2026-10-07): benign + GridSybil is the first trial of the whole pipeline (input → encoder → pretraining → evaluation). All attack types come later: DataReplay and DoS rotate pseudonyms, so their links are only 1–2 messages long and need a different window (per receiver time window) before they can be fed to the encoder.

Two data facts that govern everything downstream. (1) Windows from one sender overlap (stride 10 < window 20), so any split that is not sender-level leaks half-windows across the boundary — the committed splits are sender-level, and any new split must be too. (2) Attack labels are per-sender, not per-window — an attacker's individual windows can look locally benign, which is both the motivation for sequence modeling and a source of label noise the thesis must disclose (plan 6.6).
Later finding F1 — the v1 split leaks across scenarios. The 4 attack scenarios reuse the same SUMO traffic, so a benign vehicle appears in all 4 folders at almost the same positions. The v1 split is sender-disjoint within a scenario but not across them: 89.7% of benign test identities (2,283 / 2,546) have a near-copy in train. This is why the adopted prep uses a new split.

Why "road-network-grounded" is legitimate
Honest trajectories are road-constrained by the generator. We hold no road graph — the grounding is in the data.
If asked "is the ground truth noise-free?" No — matched messageIDs are byte-identical between ledger and receive logs. Our windows are what an eavesdropping detector sees. That is the right realism.

Sentinel
dt = dpos = dspd = 0 only at step 0 of each identity's first window (its first message has no predecessor) — ≈ 8.8% of windows. Later windows carry real deltas at step 0. Mask it out of the MTR loss.
Why deltas matter: forgeries are self-inconsistent (frozen position + moving speed; 1 km jump in 1 s). Inconsistency is learnable without labels.

Professor Q&A · data

Likely questions — one-line answers

QuestionAnswer
Is the dataset balanced?By window, binary is 50/50. By class it's up to 9.5:1 (benign : DataReplay). By identity, 61/39. I report all three and use macro-F1.
Why are attack families different sizes?Same ~1,700 attacker vehicles each; they differ in how many sustained fake identities survive the 20-message cutoff. DoS pairs are identical by construction.
Why not per-message classification?Speed/acceleration marginals are identical across 3 of 4 families (slide 8). The signal is cross-step inconsistency — a sequence model's job.
Is it real data?Simulated (SUMO/LuST + VEINS). Realistic road constraints, but smoother than telemetry — stated as a limitation; *_noise channels reserved for a robustness check.
How do you know there's no leakage?Identity-level splits, zero shared identities on every pair, train-only normalization — all re-verified by scripts/audit_splits.py. Plus pretraining is restricted to idx_train.
Why is 0709 twice the size of 1416?Morning traffic density (~60k vs ~30k msgs/bin). Attack share is steady in both — so transfer measures context robustness.
Any data problems?One: a pseudonym sentinel splices 653 GridSybil vehicles into 14,764 windows (5.16%). Found in Phase 1, fix scheduled before any cited run.
Professor Q&A · method & labels (adopted plan)

Likely questions — one-line answers

QuestionAnswer
You say no labels — where do labels appear at all?Pretraining reads no attack label: its only labels are the violations we inject ourselves (P1–P3). Attack labels score the frozen encoder (probes, AUROC) and, in few-shot fine-tuning, fit n ∈ {10, 20, 30, 50} examples per class.
Doesn't pretraining on attack windows teach the model that attacks are normal?It sees them as sequences, yes. Detection rests on physical consistency: P1–P3 learn what injected violations look like, and augmentations are kept physically consistent so the encoder is not trained to ignore attack artifacts (sensitivity probe S1.3.C5).
What is the zero-shot protocol?A memory bank of unlabeled training embeddings; score = mean cosine similarity to the K nearest; K and θ chosen on val, test read once. A17 DataReplay is held out of fine-tuning. (A benign-only bank would need labels.)
Why TimesNet, and not a transformer?The advisor's plan: FFT periods + 2-D convolutions over T = 64 messages. Vehicle motion is mostly aperiodic, so the Transformer with the same inputs, outputs and pretraining is run as an ablation — that comparison answers whether the period modelling helps.
How will you handle imbalance without labels?You can't in pretraining — and needn't. Windows are padded to 64 with a mask, so masked pooling cannot see the length (length alone: AUC 0.531); imbalance is controlled at the head (balanced probe, n per class) and in metrics (macro-F1, per-class recall).
Which family will be hardest?DataReplay (rarest, small offsets, rotating pseudonyms that rarely reach T = 64 — a documented blind spot). DoSDisruptive's timing signature is now covered by the log-Δτ input and its reconstruction target.
What is "road-network-grounded" if you have no road graph?No map is used: positions are flat SUMO metres, hard negatives are 50 m grid cells, and P1–P3 need no map. Grounding is implicit (honest motion follows LuST roads). OSM alignment is an optional test that needs a download OK.
